Business Context

MPHA turns healthcare operating signals into governed decisions and guided action

Metro Public Health Authority needs to reduce claims leakage, improve decision speed, explain provider and district risk, and convert policy guidance into operational action. This workshop demonstrates an Agentic AI with Human-in-Loop pattern where operational signals become trusted data products, analytics, AI explanations, and recommended actions through Oracle AI Data Platform, Autonomous AI Lakehouse, Oracle Analytics Cloud, machine learning, and AI agents.

MPHA business problems

  • Claims transparency and denial leakage across districts, programs, and claim types.
  • Disbursement visibility where submitted amounts do not convert into paid amounts.
  • Membership, provider accreditation, service-area, and facility-pressure accountability.
  • Policy guidance that is difficult to operationalize from long playbook documents.
  • Disconnected data and AI workflows that slow down investigation and action.

Workshop capabilities

  • OAC Claims Executive Overview with KPIs, trends, hotspots, maps, and Assistant responses.
  • Claims star schema in Autonomous AI Lakehouse as the trusted Gold serving layer.
  • Round 2 JSON and spatial context extension for richer district-level Claims insights.
  • Denial risk scoring to prioritize claims that need review or intervention.
  • Claims and Policy Copilot that combines SQL analytics with RAG over the MPHA playbook.

Signal to action story

SignalClaims, membership, disbursement, provider, JSON event, spatial, and document inputs.
Trusted dataAIDP Bronze, Silver, and Gold layers create reusable governed data products.
InsightAI Lakehouse Claims star schema powers OAC KPIs, trends, hotspots, and drilldowns.
ExplanationOAC Assistant and the copilot explain drivers using the same trusted layer.
RecommendationSQL and RAG combine claims evidence with MPHA playbook guidance.
ActionParticipants produce a claims review, provider outreach, or district escalation brief.

Lab 0

Prepare the workshop environment and participant handoff

Admin-led · 45-60 minutes
Admin step

The facilitator prepares the environment once so first-time participants are not distracted by tenancy, policy, user, bucket, catalog, and service provisioning details. Do not move to Lab 1 until these setup steps are complete and visible to the participants.

Personas and value moment Platform admin, data engineer, and governance owner prepare the managed foundation so participants can focus on the business flow from signal to action instead of tenancy plumbing.

Complete these steps in order. Each step includes the checkpoint screenshot that confirms the expected screen or result before moving forward.

Admin step

After creating the prefixes, return to the bucket view and confirm the folders are visible before moving to AIDP volumes.

Lab 4

Train, test, evaluate, score, and register an ML model

Participant hands-on · Required

Predict high-denial segment-months using prior-calendar-month outcomes and known segment identities. This is a small synthetic-data teaching baseline, not an individual claims eligibility model.

Step 1: Open the corrected ML notebook

  1. Open 05_ML_Claims_Train_Test_Eval.ipynb from your named folder, or restore it from the current execution pack.
  2. Set participant_id and output_base from your handoff sheet. Verify gold_stage/gold_claims_summary exists from notebook 03.
  3. Attach the approved Intel/AMD Spark compute. If MLflow is unavailable, ask the administrator to initialize Experiments and coordinate a restart; do not silently replace MLflow tracking.
  4. Run the notebook from top to bottom. It creates MPHA_<participant_id>_Claims_Denial_Risk through MLflow, with a new run for each execution.

Step 2: Check temporal splits before trusting evaluation

Inspect the printed counts: 622 source rows, 303 February-April training rows, 110 May test rows, and 102 June scoring rows. January supplies lag history only. Train labels: 50 positive / 253 negative; May labels: 19 positive / 91 negative. Preprocessing and logistic regression fit only on training rows. Current-month denial, approval and payment outcomes are not predictors.

Step 3: Inspect the finished experiment and fitted artifact

  1. Open Experiments, select your experiment, then the newest prior_month_logistic_baseline run.
  2. Confirm status FINISHED. A FAILED run with metrics is not completion.
  3. In Overview and Artifacts verify claims_denial_pipeline and evaluation.json. This is the actual fitted pipeline, not a substitute weighted formula.

Step 4: Evaluate quality rather than accuracy alone

Open Metrics. The reference run has test ROC AUC 0.663, precision 0.400, recall 0.105, F1 0.167, accuracy 0.818 and Brier 0.137 versus baseline 0.143. Confusion matrix: [[88, 3], [17, 2]]. Low recall means most high-denial examples are missed at threshold 0.5. Record this limitation; do not claim production readiness or tune repeatedly on the held-out test set.

Step 4: Configure the Claims star schema SQL tool

  1. Select SQL_1. Choose the assigned external catalog and participant schema.
  2. Use SQL-query configuration and paste the fixed query below. This exercise has no TOP_N parameter or missing input binding.
  3. Keep the query read-only. Test SQL_1 with attached AI compute and confirm five rows, including service month and submitted/denied counts.
  4. If access fails, verify the catalog/schema and ask the admin to refresh metadata or correct scoped grants. Do not connect as ADMIN.
SELECT
  d.full_date AS service_month,
  di.district_name,
  p.coverage_program,
  c.claim_type,
  SUM(f.claims_submitted) AS claims_submitted,
  SUM(f.denied_claims) AS denied_claims,
  ROUND((SUM(f.denied_claims) * 100.0) /
        CASE WHEN SUM(f.claims_submitted) = 0 THEN NULL ELSE SUM(f.claims_submitted) END, 2) AS denial_rate_pct,
  ROUND(SUM(f.total_submitted_amount), 2) AS total_submitted_amount,
  ROUND(SUM(f.total_paid_amount), 2) AS total_paid_amount,
  ROUND(SUM(f.avg_processing_days * f.claims_submitted) / NULLIF(SUM(f.claims_submitted), 0), 1) AS avg_processing_days
FROM mpha_fact_claims_monthly f
JOIN mpha_dim_date d ON f.service_month_date_key = d.date_key
JOIN mpha_dim_district di ON f.district_key = di.district_key
JOIN mpha_dim_coverage_program p ON f.program_key = p.program_key
JOIN mpha_dim_claim_type c ON f.claim_type_key = c.claim_type_key
GROUP BY d.full_date, di.district_name, p.coverage_program, c.claim_type
ORDER BY denial_rate_pct DESC, denied_claims DESC, d.full_date, di.district_name, p.coverage_program, c.claim_type
FETCH FIRST 5 ROWS ONLY
